Quantitative research note · 31 August 2026

The alpha was there the whole time. The portfolio couldn't see it.

A machine-learning swing model on 76 US large caps predicts 5-day residual returns with an out-of-sample information coefficient of +0.0240 (t = 3.86), and it replicates on an independent 482-name universe at +0.0255 (t = 4.11). Traded dollar-neutral it loses money. Traded neutral to the same factor space the prediction is defined in, it earns a net Sharpe of 0.74. That gap — not the signal — is the finding.

Part one · What the existing model gets wrong

Four structural problems, none of them sloppiness

NebulaQuant is better engineered than most retail quant repos. It has genuine purged walk-forward splits, ATR-scaled triple-barrier labels, and López de Prado uniqueness weights — the author clearly knows the literature. The failures are architectural, which is why more tuning was never going to fix them. The config file records the outcome plainly: the ML theses "failed the edge gate" and the project pivoted to a rules-based momentum screen.

01

Raw price levels are fed to the model Leakage-adjacent

feature_columns() returns every column not on a deny-list, which sweeps in sma_20, sma_50, sma_200, atr_14, macd, macd_signal, vol_ma_20 and dollar_volume — all in raw dollars.

This is not future leakage, which is why it survives a purged split. It is worse in a subtle way: sma_200 ∈ [310, 315] is a near-unique fingerprint for one name in one era. A gradient-boosted tree will happily learn name-and-era-specific rules that carry zero forward information, and those rules expire silently as prices drift. Every feature in the replacement is a within-date cross-sectional rank, which cannot be memorised this way.

02

The target is absolute, so the model predicts the market Root cause

tb_label asks whether a long entered tomorrow hits a 2×ATR profit target before a 1×ATR stop. That outcome is dominated by market direction: across 76 correlated large caps, mean pairwise return correlation in this sample is 0.267. Most of the variance the model can explain is beta.

So the model learned to be a market timer wearing a stock-picker's clothes. That is exactly what the pivot to "regime-gated momentum" — SPY above its 200-day average, VIX below its 85th percentile — formalised. Residualising returns first drops that correlation to −0.011, which is what makes genuine cross-sectional selection possible.

03

Three positions is not enough bets to measure skill Fatal for inference

execution.target_positions: 3 with a 20-day hold gives roughly 13 independent bets per year. The fundamental law of active management puts information ratio at IC × √breadth; at that breadth, an annual result is essentially a coin-flip sequence. You cannot distinguish a good model from a lucky quarter, in either direction.

04

Hyperparameters are selected on the reported folds Inflates results

optimize.py runs 20 Optuna trials scored on the median Sharpe across the walk-forward test folds, tuning depth, learning rate, probability threshold and both barrier multiples. Once a fold has been used to choose a configuration it is in-sample. Only the 15% holdout stays clean, and at roughly 440 days that is far too small to carry a 20-trial search. The replacement fixes hyperparameters a priori and never tunes them.

A bias worth naming out loud The universe is 76 tickers chosen in 2026 because they are today's liquid large caps. Every one of them survived and most thrived over the sample. For a long-only backtest that is close to disqualifying. For the cross-sectional market-neutral strategy studied here the distortion is much smaller — the book is long and short inside the same survivor set — but it is not zero, and no result below should be read as free of it.
Part two · Where an edge could plausibly come from

Four published results, and what each one demands you build

Short-horizon reversal is the natural candidate for a 2–10 day equity strategy: it has a mechanism rather than just a backtest. You are paid for supplying liquidity to someone who needs to trade now. But the literature is equally clear that the naive version is untradeable, and each paper points at a specific construction requirement.

A

Naive reversal dies on costs — so restrict the universe

Novy-Marx & Velikov price 23 anomalies with Hasbrouck effective spreads. Short-term reversal goes from +0.37%/month gross to −1.28%/month net (t = −6.02). Industry-relative reversal: +0.98% gross, −0.80% net. Their universe is all of CRSP, where the microcaps that generate most of the gross return carry enormous spreads. In mega caps, spreads are one to three basis points — roughly thirty times cheaper. The anomaly is measured where it cannot be traded and traded where it is not measured.

B

Reversal mixes a winning leg with a losing one — so residualise

Da, Liu & Schaumburg decompose reversal profits: the across-industry component is worth −0.295%/month (t = −4.15) while the within-industry component is worth +0.821%/month (t = 5.49). Sorting on the discount-rate-news residual instead of raw returns lifts the three-factor alpha from 0.33% (t = 1.37) to 1.34% (t = 9.28). Close-to-close reversal bolts a losing leg onto a winning one.

C

The payoff is conditional on stress — so give the model regime context

Nagel shows normalised VIX predicts next-day reversal-strategy returns with a coefficient of 0.22 (t ≈ 11) and an adjusted R² of 0.07 daily, rising to 0.56 monthly — extraordinary for a predictive return regression. Liquidity provision is paid most when liquidity is scarce.

D

Overnight and intraday returns carry opposite signs — so split them

Lou, Polk & Skouras find momentum accrues entirely overnight (0.98%/month, t = 3.84) and nothing intraday (−0.02%, t = −0.06). Short-term reversal earns +0.93% overnight and −1.05% intraday. A close-to-close signal averages those two into mush.

The resulting design brief is specific: a large-cap universe where costs are survivable, a signal defined on factor-residual returns, regime variables available to the model as interaction context, an overnight/intraday decomposition, and turnover control through overlapping tranches rather than a full daily rebalance.

Part three · Method

What was built, and what stops it cheating

Universe
76 US large caps, 68–76 names per date, 2017-01 → 2026-08 (177,709 stock-days)
Risk model
Rolling equal-weight market factor + top-4 PCs of the market-residual covariance. Betas fit on a trailing 252 days, refit every 21 days, held fixed and applied forward.
Features
42 stock-level, each a within-date percentile rank; 8 date-level regime variables left raw as interaction context.
Target
Within-date rank of the forward 5-day residual return.
Model
LightGBM regression, 15 leaves, depth 4, L2 = 10, min_child_samples = 200. Averaged over 3 seeds. Hyperparameters fixed a priori and never tuned.
Validation
Expanding walk-forward, 63-day OOS blocks, 3-year minimum train, purge = 5-day label horizon + 5-day embargo. 27 blocks, 1,666 OOS days.
Execution
Signal at the close of day t, fill at the close of t+1, returns earned t+2 … t+6. A full day of implementation lag.
Costs
2.5 bp one-way baseline plus 50 bp/yr borrow on short notional; sensitivity run from 0 to 20 bp.

Three checks were run before any model was fit. Data QA across all 77 raw files found no NaN closes, no duplicate dates, no OHLC violations and no unadjusted splits — the two flagged single-day moves above 35% (ORCL, SNOW) are real earnings reactions. Open, high and low were rescaled onto the dividend-adjusted basis; skipping that step injects a spurious −0.5% overnight gap on every ex-dividend date, which would have poisoned the overnight/intraday split. And the factor model does what it claims: mean pairwise correlation falls from 0.267 on raw returns to −0.011 on residuals.

Part four · What the raw signals say

Reversal alone is weak. That is the expected answer.

Before combining anything, each feature was tested on its own: daily cross-sectional Spearman IC against the forward 5-day residual return, with Newey-West standard errors at lag 10 to absorb the overlap in the labels.

Univariate information coefficients, 2017–2026

Positive IC means the signal ranks winners correctly. Reversal signals are stated so that positive = past losers outperform.

SignalICNW tIRHit rate

This matches the literature rather than contradicting it. Reversal is strongest in small, illiquid names and has decayed substantially since decimalisation; in a 76-name mega-cap cross-section it should be marginal, and it is. Conditioning on volatility helps in the predicted direction — 5-day reversal IC rises from 0.0047 in the lowest VIX tercile to 0.0183 in the highest, roughly Nagel's result — but not enough to carry a strategy alone.

The strongest single signal is 12-1 residual momentum (IC 0.0244, t = 3.53). Two apparently strong signals were tested for whether they are alpha or artefact by splitting the IC series on the direction of the forward market return. Reversal flips sign (+0.026 when the market rises, −0.019 when it falls); momentum does not (+0.025 / +0.023). Reversal in this universe carries residual market exposure; momentum is closer to genuine selection.

The result that reframed the project Measured against forward raw returns instead of residual returns, almost nothing survives: every reversal variant falls below t = 1.0. A signal can be strongly predictive in residual space and worth nothing in raw space. Which space you trade in is therefore not a detail of implementation — it is the strategy.
Part five · The central result

Same signal, same costs, four ways to build the book

The model is trained to predict returns orthogonal to a five-dimensional factor space: market beta plus four principal components. A book that is merely dollar- and beta-neutral still carries loadings on PC2 through PC4, and those loadings generate variance an order of magnitude larger than the alpha. The forecast is drowned by risk the forecast said nothing about.

The fix is to construct weights in the space the prediction lives in — project the signal orthogonal to the point-in-time factor loadings, so the realised P&L is the residual return the model forecasts:

w  ∝  (I − B (B'B)⁻¹ B') s

Net Sharpe by neutralisation level

Identical predictions, identical 2.5 bp costs, identical 5-day overlapping tranches. The only thing that changes is which factors the portfolio is neutral to.

This is the direct answer to why the original model failed. It predicted per-stock absolute outcomes and traded an unhedged long-only book, so its forecasts and its risk lived in different spaces. No amount of feature engineering closes that gap; only construction does.

The broad universe also recovers the reversal effect that the mega-cap universe could not resolve. In 482 names, 1-day reversal reaches t = 2.61, 5-day t = 2.25 and vol-scaled 5-day t = 2.20, against roughly 1.6–1.9 in the 76-name set. Caveats on the validation data The 482-name file carries no dividend adjustment, so its overnight component is contaminated on ex-dividend dates, and its membership is S&P 500 as of 2018, so it is survivorship-biased in the same direction as the primary set. It is a genuine independent test of breadth and period, not of survivorship.
Part eight · Trying to kill it

Five tests designed to make the result go away

1. Null distributions

40 draws each. The permutation null shuffles the model's own predictions across tickers within each date, preserving every distributional property of the signal and destroying only its alignment with returns.

2. Deflated Sharpe ratio 0.918

Bailey & López de Prado's correction for the fact that an observed Sharpe is the maximum of many trials.

Charging honestly for 33 configurations examined across this study (15 hold-period × weight-cap combinations, 8 candidate signals, 6 cost levels, 4 neutralisation variants), the probability that the true Sharpe exceeds zero is 0.918. That is suggestive, not conclusive — it falls short of the conventional 0.95 bar. Reported as-is rather than by quietly lowering the trial count.

3. Probability of backtest overfitting PBO = 0.690

Combinatorially symmetric cross-validation over 15 configurations, 10 time blocks.

PBO above 0.5 nominally means "the selection procedure is worse than random." The honest reading here is narrower, and the reason is visible in the numbers: all 15 configurations produce Sharpes between +0.518 and +0.779. When every variant performs about equally well, which one ranks first in any given sample is close to arbitrary, and PBO climbs toward 0.5 and beyond by construction.

So this statistic says do not bother optimising the holding period or the weight cap — there is nothing to gain and the apparent gains will not persist. It does not say the strategy is spurious; a spurious strategy would show a wide config spread with some variants negative. Every configuration tested is positive.

5. Feature-block ablation

Each block removed in turn, the full walk-forward re-run from scratch.

Momentum is load-bearing: removing it costs 0.40 Sharpe points, more than everything else combined. Regime context is worth 0.09, confirming that the conditional structure Nagel documents is real and that the model is exploiting it. Reversal, liquidity and the overnight/intraday split each contribute a modest 0.03–0.04.

The risk/volatility block is negative: dropping its ten features raises net Sharpe from 0.739 to 0.823. Those features are slow-moving cross-sectional tilts — beta, idiosyncratic volatility, skew, kurtosis — that mostly duplicate exposures the portfolio construction already neutralises, so they add estimation noise without adding information.

Do not bank the 0.823 Acting on that ablation is itself a selection decision made after seeing out-of-sample results. The pre-registered full model scores 0.739 and that is the number to plan against. The lean variant is a hypothesis for the next independent test, not a free upgrade.
Part nine · What this is not

The honest ledger

A 0.74 Sharpe market-neutral strategy earning 3.1% a year on 4.2% volatility is a real but modest result. Here is everything that should temper it.

It needs leverage to matter

Unlevered, this returns 3.1% a year — below cash for much of the sample. Getting to a 10% return needs roughly 3× gross leverage, which means a margin account, financing costs that the 50 bp/yr borrow assumption only partly captures, and the discipline to hold through a 15% levered drawdown.

It is operationally heavy for a retail account

The book carries 57 positions above 0.5% with 0.89 gross exposure and turns over 40× a year. That is roughly 20 trades a day, long and short, with fractional shares and a short locate on every name. This is not a strategy you run by hand.

The statistical case is suggestive, not settled

t = 2.18 over 1,666 days. Deflated Sharpe 0.918, below the 0.95 bar. Neither subperiod is individually significant. The replication is what carries the most weight here — two independent universes agreeing on IC to within 0.0015 is harder to produce by chance than either result alone — but this is evidence for a small edge, not proof of a large one.

Survivorship bias is present in both samples

Both universes are defined by present-day membership. The market-neutral construction mutes this substantially but does not remove it. A point-in-time constituent file with delisted names is the single highest-value data purchase available, and until it exists every number here has an unquantified upward tilt.

The 5-day horizon has no dividend or corporate-action model

Prices are total-return adjusted, but earnings dates, index adds and deletes, secondary offerings and M&A are unmodelled. At a 5-day horizon these are the events most likely to produce the large adverse residuals the model cannot see coming.

Part ten · What to do next

Ranked by expected value per hour spent

01

Widen the universe to 400–500 names

The highest-return change available, and the evidence is already in hand: the same pipeline on 482 names produced less than half the volatility for the same IC, and lifted net Sharpe from 0.74 to 1.22. Breadth is the one lever here with a theoretical guarantee behind it. It also moves the book toward the mid-cap names where reversal actually lives.

02

Buy point-in-time data with delisted names

Sharadar or Norgate, roughly $50–100/month. This is the only way to remove the one bias that no modelling choice can correct, and it converts every result in this note from "suggestive" to "measured."

03

Test the lean feature set out-of-sample

The ablation says the risk/volatility block hurts. Confirm or reject that on the wider universe before adopting it — and register the decision before looking, so it is a test rather than another selection.

04

Paper-trade for a quarter, and track IC rather than P&L

Sixty days of P&L on a 0.74 Sharpe strategy tells you nothing — the noise dwarfs the signal. Daily cross-sectional IC converges far faster. If live IC runs materially below 0.024, the difference is implementation, not the model: check fill prices against the assumed close, and turnover against the assumed 40×.

05

Do not optimise the holding period or the weight cap

PBO of 0.69 across a config spread of 0.518–0.779 is a direct instruction. Every configuration works about equally well; picking the best one is fitting noise. Leave them at 5 days and 8%.

Appendix · Running it

The pipeline

Delivered into nebulaquant-volatility-ai/research/, alongside the existing project rather than replacing it. Six modules, each independently runnable.

src/panel.py      load raw OHLCV, rescale OHL onto the total-return
                  basis, run data-integrity QA
src/factors.py    rolling point-in-time market + PCA risk model;
                  emits residual returns, betas and PC loadings
src/features.py   42 cross-sectionally ranked stock features,
                  8 raw regime features, rank targets
src/model.py      purged + embargoed expanding walk-forward;
                  fixed hyperparameters, multi-seed averaged
src/portfolio.py  factor-neutral weight construction and
                  vectorised overlapping tranches
src/stats.py      Newey-West t-stats, deflated Sharpe, PBO/CSCV

python3 build_dataset.py    # panel -> residuals -> features
python3 train_wf.py         # walk-forward, reports OOS IC
python3 run_backtest2.py    # costed portfolio + baselines
python3 robustness.py       # nulls, DSR, PBO, subperiods
python3 run_val.py          # independent 482-name replication
python3 ablation.py         # leave-one-block-out

The two pieces most worth reading are src/portfolio.py, which contains the projection that makes the whole thing work, and src/stats.py, which contains the tests that try to prove it doesn't.
